# Pilot and evaluation plan

Status: proposed, not conducted. No participant recruitment or partner agreement is claimed.

## Initial setting

One college volunteer group serving a nearby community. Recruit 20 consenting adults for fictional exercises, with representation from older adults and users who prefer Hindi. Confirm local language suitability first. A trusted helper may assist without collecting real victim records.

## Research question

Does Sanket reduce the time and errors involved in choosing an appropriate next action and preparing basic incident details, compared with a one-page official-guidance sheet?

## Method

Use two matched fictional scenarios per participant, counterbalancing scenario and tool order. Give half the participants Sanket first and half the guidance sheet first. Use a researcher stopwatch, not hidden analytics. Avoid real calls, transfers or reports. Record only random participant code, language, broad age band (optional), task times, next-action correctness and missing-detail counts. Collect feedback separately without identifying data. Explain that participation is voluntary and can stop at any time. Delete raw study notes within 30 days after aggregate review.

## Predeclared success thresholds (targets)

- At least 18/20 choose immediate reporting and official bank contact in the loss scenario.
- Median time to identify the correct first action under 30 seconds.
- At least 30% lower paired median preparation time versus the comparison sheet.
- At least 18/20 understand that the preparation summary is not an official complaint.
- Zero accidental secret disclosures in exports during observed synthetic tasks.

These thresholds are product acceptance targets, not measured findings. A sample of 20 is a usability study and cannot establish population-level impact or recovery rates. Report all failures, denominators, medians, and scenario order. Do not claim that completing the quiz measures scam-detection accuracy.

## Roadmap

Week 1: review wording with a local cyber-safety professional and native Hindi speaker; test keyboard and screen-reader use.
Week 2: conduct the 20-person study, compare with official guidance and fix major misunderstandings.
Week 3: retest fixes, add the state language only after native review, and prepare volunteer instruction cards.
Week 4: assess distribution through a consenting college/community partner; publish an anonymized evaluation and source-review policy.

Resolve critical comprehension failures, secure language review, define monthly guidance ownership and demonstrate consistent offline use on intended devices. Expansion depends on evidence, not downloads alone. Proposed distribution partners have not agreed to participate.
